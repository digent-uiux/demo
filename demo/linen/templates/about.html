Field notes · Why linen

It gets better every summer you wear it

Flax needs no irrigation and very little else to grow. Woven loose, it lets air through on the hottest days, and each wash takes the stiffness out until the shirt feels like it was always yours.

Woven slowly, by the river

Our cloth comes from one family mill two hours inland. The flax is retted in the field, spun nearby and woven on old shuttle looms that run at half the speed of new ones. Slower looms leave a softer, tighter edge and a cloth that gets better with every wash.

We buy whole bolts each spring and cut only what you order, so nothing sits in a warehouse.

Four ways we keep it slow

Small habits from the workroom, written down so we never drop them.

  1. 1

    Only plant fibres

    Flax linen, raw cotton and a little hemp. Nothing spun from oil.

  2. 2

    Washed twice

    Every piece is washed and dried before it ships, so it never shrinks on you.

  3. 3

    Dried on the line

    No tumble dryers in the workroom. The roof terrace does the job in summer.

  4. 4

    Mended for life

    Send back a torn seam or a lost button, at any age, and we fix it free.
