From the garden roomSpring, 2026

Why we still grow our own calendula.

Dear reader,

Twelve years ago I had eczema on both hands and a cupboard of creams that stung. I started infusing calendula petals in sunflower oil on my kitchen table, because it was the one thing my grandmother swore by. It worked well enough that friends began to ask for jars.

We are fourteen people now, with a small lab and a half-acre walled garden. From June to September we pick the calendula every third morning, when the heads are fully open, and steep them for six weeks before a single gram goes into a formula.

With care,

Margot · Founder and head formulator

P.S. The calendula balm from this summer's harvest is batch 26-07. It's the one I keep by the sink.

The apothecary

Four things we hold to

The rules the workshop was built on. They are printed inside every lid, so you read them each time you open a jar.

IVPrinted inside every lid
  1. I

    Three farms, by name

    Oat, olive and calendula from growers we visit each harvest. The farm is printed under every lot number.

  2. II

    Small batches

    Forty jars at a time, poured and dated by hand, so nothing sits in a warehouse for a year.

  3. III

    Nothing to hide

    Full formulas, with percentages, on the label and online. No fragrance blends, no fillers.

  4. IV

    Glass, refilled

    Every jar comes back for a refill or is washed and reused. Return five, the sixth is on us.

How we count +

Natural origin follows ISO 16128 and is weighed per batch, then averaged across formulas. Customers are unique delivery addresses, not accounts. Refills are counted at the wash bench. Every figure is checked each January against the batch book.
