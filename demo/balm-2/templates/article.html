Ingredient file No. 07

Calendula: what a petal infusion does for redness

By Iris Hale·Sep 9, 2026·5 min read

Calendula is the plainest flower in our garden, and the hardest working. Steeped slowly in oil, its petals give up the compounds that make a hot, blotchy cheek settle down. Nothing about it is new: it has been an apothecary staple for centuries. What matters is how patiently it is made.

Why it calms

The petals carry triterpenoids and flavonoids that quiet the skin's response to wind, heat and over-scrubbing. In an oil infusion they stay gentle: there is no alcohol to sting, and no fragrance to set off a reaction of its own.

It won't erase rosacea or replace a dermatologist. It is a soft, daily kindness for skin that flushes easily, and it works best sealed in, at night, under a balm.

“We don't hurry the oil. Six weeks on a warm shelf is the whole recipe.”

Mara Quint · founder, at the bench

How we make it

  1. No. 01

    Dry the petals

    Calendula heads are picked in the morning and dried flat on linen for a week, until they rustle.

  2. No. 02

    Cover them in oil

    The petals go into amber jars, topped with cold-pressed sunflower oil, a finger's width over the flowers.

  3. No. 03

    Wait six weeks

    The jars sit on a warm shelf, out of the sun, and get a slow turn each morning. The oil goes from straw to gold.

  4. No. 04

    Strain and fold in

    We strain through muslin, rest the oil for two days, then fold it into the balm and the serum by hand.

At home, press two drops of the serum into damp skin, then warm a pea of balm between your palms and hold it over the flushed areas for a few breaths. Patch test first if your skin reacts to daisies.
